Background From an Iowa research lab to the field

It all started
at the river.

HydroHeight is a low-cost way to measure water level with a camera. I began building it during the University of Iowa’s Secondary Student Training Program, working in the University of Iowa Hydroinformatics Laboratory ↗ with Professor Yusuf Sermet and Professor Ibrahim Demir.

The prototype replaced a conventional stream gauge with a Raspberry Pi 4, camera, GPS, and an inertial measurement unit. A computer-vision model segments the waterline where it meets a known structure; the camera’s coordinates and orientation then turn that point in the image into a physical elevation.

The work grew from a research prototype into part of a much larger line of camera-based water monitoring. The underlying approach developed by Sermet and Demir was ultimately protected by a University of Iowa patent.↗ I took the idea one step further by adapting the workflow into a phone app, making it possible to collect an image, location, and device pose on the go.
